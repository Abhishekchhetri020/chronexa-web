## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: genuinely authored for this product — not category-interchangeable.**

This survives the swap test structurally, not cosmetically. The hero is a real timetable instrument: `landing_lattice.js` defines `DAYS = ["MON"…"SAT"]`, a 6-column × 9-row field, and every tile draws live data — subject code plus `class · period`. Tiles carry machined top hairlines, side faces, offset shadows and cyan guide rails, so they read as schedule cells in a shared field. The scene runs a constraint narrative rather than a decorative pulse: `! CLASH` → in-flight cyan card on a lifted quadratic arc → `RESOLVED`, with a dashed reroute spline, a travelling photon and a docked `CLASH DETECTED` / `AUTO-REROUTING…` / `CONSTRAINT SATISFIED` readout.

The copy is unwritable by anyone else: *"Build a timetable that behaves."* / *"A constraint is not an error message. It is part of the canvas."* / *"Only relevant targets react; valid placements answer in cyan."* The demo band is a second independent timetable artifact with a real double-booked cell. Every prohibition in the approved brief is honoured — no stock imagery, no gradient text, no purple accent, no fabricated metrics, no glass, no decorative blur.

Two specificity leaks undercut it: a fabricated "FET" capability claim, and positionally invented tile labels producing classes up to `16A` — the exact "fabricated data" the product's own rules forbid.

**Deterministic scan: 13 findings, all false positives — and the scan itself was DEGRADED.**

`detect.mjs --json index.html` exited 2 with 13 findings: 10 × `design-system-color`, 1 × `design-system-font`, 1 × `design-system-font-size`, 1 × `design-system-radius` — every one in `index.html` at lines 25, 41, 376–387. All 376–387 are inside a single inline JS string building the "Lessons" modal, not landing markup. `#fff` and `#f8fafc` are Tailwind neutrals; `Helvetica Neue` is a deliberate fallback-stack entry; line 25 is the FOUC guard. **None of the 13 touches the hero CSS.**

The run printed: *"HTML parser modules unavailable (htmlparser2, css-select, css-tree, domutils). Falling back to regex matching… findings are an undercount, not a clean bill of health."* Treat 13 as a floor; the scan evaluated no contrast, no custom properties and no selector matching — which is precisely why the two worst defects on this page (broken webfonts, sub-AA tile labels) appear nowhere in it.

**Visual overlays: not delivered.** Injection preflight passed (no CSP; `document.title` and an appended `<script>` both took effect), but loading `http://localhost:8400/detect.js` into the HTTPS page failed with `net::ERR_FAILED` — mixed content. **No user-visible overlay exists.** The live server was stopped and verified (port free, no process, repo clean).

## Overall Impression

A confident, specific, genuinely well-built landing page that is being undermined by defects that have nothing to do with its design idea. The composition is the strongest thing here and it is not the problem. The problems are: the page renders in the wrong typeface everywhere and says nothing about it, the first 1.55 seconds show a different product, the headline collides with its own hero canvas below 1024px, and a fabricated format claim sits in the one sentence aimed at the actual buyer.

**Single biggest opportunity:** the hero is a beautiful claim, but nothing on the page lets a coordinator test it on their own data. The drop handler and the board already exist and simply are not connected.

## What's Working

**1. The lattice is the argument, not the decoration.** A genuine six-day × nine-period field with day headers, period coordinates and section labels, running a labelled conflict → reroute → resolve machine. The thesis becomes something you watch happen instead of something you are told.

**2. The three-entry-path hierarchy is correct and honestly measured.** At 1504×1046: `Open timetable` 184×48 filled mineral-white; `Launch demo` 126×48 dark with a hairline; `Start with a blank school` 138×25 underlined. Visual weight ordering matches decision importance exactly, and the two real actions hold 48px on mobile.

**3. Reduced motion is engineered, not tokenised.** rAF drops to 0, the lattice renders once settled, the entrance choreography is disabled, the demo shows its final frame with zero running animations and hides the loop control. Verified independently by both assessments.

**4. The no-WebGL fallback works — confirmed by parent verification.** With `getContext` forced to null for all WebGL types, the page renders a complete, legible line-art lattice: all copy, both CTAs, day headers, every subject label and period coordinate still readable, correctly static. Assessment B claimed this path produces a "grey, label-only hero" and "visually dead" scene; that claim is **wrong** — B's own artifact `/tmp/critB/shots/v-nowebgl-1504x1046.png` is byte-for-byte the same render as A's, and both are fully legible. B's automated colour-saturation metric measured the absence of tile *fills*, which is the intended degradation, not a failure. **This is the best-engineered edge case on the page.**

## Priority Issues

### [P0] What: All three brand fonts silently fail site-wide

**Why it matters.** `css/design-v3.css:9` imports Fraunces, Inter Tight and JetBrains Mono in one Google Fonts URL. That URL returns **HTTP 400** (`content-type: text/html`), which the browser rejects as `net::ERR_BLOCKED_BY_ORB`. One malformed family kills the shared import, so **all three** fall back to system fonts. Bisected: the `SOFT,WONK` custom axes on the Fraunces clause are the culprit — removing them returns HTTP 200. Parent-verified: `document.fonts.size === 0`, and rendered text width is byte-identical to the generic fallback (`"Fraunces", serif` = 790.88px vs `serif` = 790.88px; same for Inter Tight and JetBrains Mono). The failure is completely silent — no console error, and CSS still *claims* the families, so every computed-style check passes. **The committed typography system in DESIGN.md is not what ships.** This is why the headline reads as a generic grotesque.

**Fix.** Strip `SOFT,WONK` from the Fraunces clause (keep `opsz,wght`), and split the import into one `@import` per family so a future single-family failure cannot take the others down. Then re-tune the hero: the current `clamp(52px, 6.7vw, 96px)` and `-0.04em` were speced against Inter Tight's metrics and will need re-checking once it actually renders.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P0] What: "FET" is a fabricated capability claim

**Why it matters.** `index.html:196` reads: *"Work with XML, Excel, calendar, FET, and printable timetable outputs."* Chronexa has no FET importer or exporter. The product's own help centre enumerates the true set — import: *Timetable XML, Classic Basic, GP Untis, clipboard TSV*; export: *Timetable XML, Excel (5 sheets), HTML standalone, PowerSchool, GP Untis DIF, Atlantis ROZ, ICS calendar* (`js/ui/components/help_center.js:97-98`). The only FET in the codebase is ported constraint *semantics*, not file interop. FET is a direct competitor, so this lands precisely on the buyer who came from it: they download expecting to open their file, the picker greys it out, and every other claim on the page becomes suspect. It also breaks the product's own binding no-fabrication rule.

**Fix.** Replace with the true sentence, same length: **"Open Classic/ASC XML and GP-Untis DIF. Export XML, Excel, HTML, ICS, PowerSchool, and Atlantis ROZ. FET-style constraint semantics are built in."**

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P0] What: The first 1.55 seconds show the wrong product

**Why it matters.** Measured first-paint trace: `[[1362, "rgb(245,245,247)", false], [2914, "rgb(5,7,10)", true]]`. From 1.36s to 2.91s the visitor sees the light app shell, header, six step-nav chips and a disabled amber `Generate` — then it snaps black. The brief's opening requirement is "full-bleed ink-black spatial field; **app chrome is absent**". On a throttled connection this measured 31.5s. The class is only applied when JS calls `showStep(1)`.

**Fix.** One attribute. In `index.html:44` change `<body class="bg-slate-50 …">` to `<body class="chrx-landing-active bg-slate-50 …">`. Every rule in `landing-redesign.css:3-39` then applies at first paint, and the existing `classList.toggle("chrx-landing-active", n === 1)` in `js/ui/main.js:286` removes it correctly on reaching the editor. No JS change, no new CSS.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] What: The headline is painted over by its own canvas below ~1024px

**Why it matters.** At 752×523 (≈200% zoom) the hero headline measures **1.06:1** against the worst canvas colour behind it — white tile glyphs cut straight through the type, which needs 3:1. At 640×512 it is **1.03:1**, the lead drops to 1.26:1, and the primary CTA sits at 1.03:1 where a white tile passes under it. At 1024×768 it is a hard geometric collision rather than a near-miss: the copy column is 481px but the type renders to **x = 512** while the board's leftmost tiles start at **x ≈ 378**. Desktop at 1504×1046 is clean — one painted pixel under each element, 17.69:1. There is no horizontal overflow anywhere, so this is pure overlap. Root cause: copy and canvas are both full-bleed and the breakpoints never separate them below ~1024px.

**Fix.** Below ~1100px, move the scene to its own band rather than behind the copy — let the copy sit on flat ink and the lattice occupy a bounded region below it. If the overlap is intentional, it needs a real scrim: a solid gradient behind the copy column, not the current `rgba(5,7,10,.72)` at 34% that lets saturated tiles through. Also fix the 1024 breakpoint: `landing-redesign.css:457-461` reduces the font size but never narrows the 47vw copy column or pushes the board right.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] What: The `?` dialog traps keyboard users, and the privacy promise is deleted on mobile

**Why it matters.** Two related trust failures. The `?` sits directly beside both primary CTAs — it is the highest-traffic "I'm not sure" affordance on the page. It opens a `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` that Escape does not close and focus never enters: after opening, `document.activeElement` is still `#start-sample-info`; after Escape the scrim is still visible and `elementFromPoint` on `Launch demo` returns the scrim — **the page is hard-blocked**. The `aria-modal` contract is simply not honoured. Separately, *"Your timetable stays in this browser unless you choose a cloud solve."* is the fact that unlocks institutional adoption, and it renders as 10px grey mono in the far bottom-right corner — then is `display:none` outright below 980px (`landing-redesign.css:462`), so it is absent on every tablet and phone. The capability band repeats the idea ~2,800px later, after the decision is already made.

**Fix.** In `openSampleInfo()`: focus the close button on open, add an Escape handler calling the same close path as `×` and the scrim, trap Tab, and restore focus on close. For the privacy line, delete the `display:none` and relocate it into the copy column at ≤980px (`position: static; margin-top: 14px`), and on desktop promote it to the `.chrx-lattice-copy__signal` treatment so it reads as a claim rather than a footnote — it already clears contrast at 5.89:1; prominence is the problem, not legibility.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer).** At t+1.4s sees `Editor` / `School Info` / `Class Grid` / `Teacher Grid` / `Room Grid` and a disabled amber `Generate`, and concludes this is a school ERP; black arrives 1.5s later. Then reads the biggest object — the lattice — and cannot decipher the tile labels at 2.29–4.99:1. The field reads as a colourful mosaic rather than a timetable, the exact inverse of the intended meaning. Clicks `?`, which answers a question Jordan did not ask; Escape does nothing. Clicks the one phrase that sounds like *just let me in* — "Start with a blank school" — and gets seven templates. **Abandons at the modal.**

**Riley (stress tester).** Reduced motion is correct, but `reducedMotion` forces `progress = 1` and skips the cycle clock, so the promised red-conflict-reroutes-to-cyan moment **never happens** for Riley — the page's central idea is invisible to exactly the users who need it stated statically. At 1024×768 the "e" of "timetable" sits on a MATH tile. The hero is 780px tall in a 768px viewport, so `Explore the field` — the cue that says "scroll" — is below the fold. Notices the blue glow and concludes the CSS is broken.

**Casey (distracted mobile user, 390×844).** First viewport is sound: no horizontal overflow (`scrollWidth === 390`), both CTAs at y 550–598 with 48px targets. But **every other tap target fails the design system's own 44px floor**: `?` 42×48, `Start with a blank school` 138×25, the five stage tabs 36px tall, the loop button 23px, `Docs` 25px, `Back to top` 25px. Casey taps `Conflict` and it is a coin flip. Never sees the privacy line — `display:none` below 980px.

**Project persona — academic coordinator evaluating against aSc Timetables.** The one format claim aimed at them is false, so they either distrust the page or download and bounce. They cannot learn what file to bring before clicking: nothing on the page names `.xml`. They cannot test their real data — `Launch demo` loads one fixed single-class school. They cannot verify scale: every constraint visible is a mock, and the tile labels are positionally invented, producing class `16A` when their school runs I–X. And after launching the demo, File → Import sits behind a 0×0 menubar, so a coordinator who takes the demo as far as "let me try my own file" has no visible route.

## Minor Observations

1. `SAT` column header is clipped mid-glyph at desktop — deliberate oversizing, but a half-visible day header reads as a defect, not a bleed.
2. File errors surface in `#xml-status`, a `position: fixed; bottom: 22px` toast detached from the control that failed.
3. The `pick up → read the field → land instantly` sequence loses its causality below 980px, where the connector arrows are `display:none` and the steps become a plain feature list.
4. The default solver backend is a remote host (`window.CHRONEXA_BACKEND_URL` points at a Hugging Face Space) while the hero leads with "Local-first". Not a defect — the disclosure exists — but "cloud solve" is doing a lot of work here.
5. The page self-reloads ~43s after first load on service-worker activation, which can interrupt a visitor mid-animation.
6. 276KB of the 629KB transferred is `xlsx.full.min.js` from a CDN, loaded on a landing page where no spreadsheet is being opened.

## Questions to Consider

- The page *shows* the mechanism but never lets anyone *touch* it. What if the hero lattice were itself the drop target — drag your real `timetable.xml` onto the board and watch your own classes assemble, with your own conflicts marked? The drop handler (`start_screen.js:96-107`) and the board both exist and simply are not connected. That single change replaces "beautiful claim" with "proof on my data".
- If local-first is the product's spine, why is the evidence for it the smallest, greyest text on the page — and deleted on mobile? What would the hero look like if the privacy guarantee carried the same compositional weight as the signal row?
- The brief says "one authored moment". The page ships four simultaneous motions plus an infinite blink. If exactly one survived, and the answer were "the conflict reroute, once, on arrival, then never again" — does the page get *more* persuasive?
- What would the coordinator's 30-second test be? They have one real file and one question: "will this survive 42 sections and 68 teachers?" Would a strip of real numbers from `sample-school.xml` outperform the entire proof section — and be the only true numbers on the page?
